# Taste Critic — round 06

Could this only be Pip? Mostly, yes — and that is the highest thing I have said about any app in a while. The voice is real: "a whole week, kept.", "there are 4 more days of you past the last week", "cancel anytime · export and delete stay free, always", "i'm a small app, not a person who can keep you safe right now". The mood system is colour + one line icon, never a chart. Pip changes expression across the four onboarding steps (listening → thinking → cozy → happy) instead of being a sticker. What keeps this off the top shelf is a handful of borrowed shapes (a blurred-preview paywall, an iOS settings list, a checkmark feature list) and a verbal tic — "kept" — that has started to wallpaper the product.

## Red flags
- memory-locked-paywall (light + dark): ghost card — blur used because it was possible. `MemoryDetail.tsx` renders a `blur-[3px] saturate-50` article whose body is five hard-coded skeleton bars, then a `bg-gradient-to-b from-transparent to-bg` scrim over it. The only legible thing through the blur is the date, which the paywall card could simply state. Blurring placeholder bars is theatre; it is also the most-copied freemium pattern on the store.
- onboarding-welcome, onboarding-notify (light + dark): copy block to CTA — floaty spacing. ≈340px of empty `#FFF9ED` between "help is one tap away." (y≈385) and the "hey pip" button (y≈728). On name/focus/rhythm the high content is motivated by the keyboard (`StepShell` comment); on the two screens with no input it is just a hole. Pip at 140px could be 180px and the copy block could sit lower.
- Checked and clean: gradients (Pip body/glow only, both sunlight→honey); framework spacing (94.6% on-grid, remaining values are 4px inside pills); colour count (cream + yellows + 3 brand pastels, 70/20/10 holds on 23/23 screens per gate); card grids as filler (the five "earlier" compact cards and six settings rows are real content, not decoration); generic copy (none found — closest is "just paid? tap here"); glassmorphism (none; `Sheet` uses a plain `ink/40` backdrop); type balance (Fraunces 32/24/18 ramp reads as one keepsake voice, Fredoka only at 32/18).

## Top 3 fixes (highest leverage first)
1. Replace the blurred ghost on the locked memory with an honest object: a plain surface card showing the real date and "you wrote 2 things this day" in Fraunces, no blur, no scrim, then the paywall card. It says the same thing without the trope, and it is true.
2. Thin "kept" to two uses per screen. On timeline: keep "a whole week, kept." and "kept by pip"; change the header line to "7 days · last 7 days" and the card footer to "2 things you said" (which also sets up the detail's "what i said, that day"). Rename the subscription line "your last seven days, kept" → "the last seven days".
3. Make the pause screen quieter than the thread: demote "i feel ready" to the `soft` variant (`surface-2` fill), drop "even breathing · switch" to a text link reading "try a longer out-breath", and soften `--ease-spring` to `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.2, 0.64, 1)` app-wide so bubbles settle instead of bounce.

## Notes for peers
- Systems Designer: count the radii — 12/16/20/24px across `Skeleton`/`SettingRow`/`rounded-card`/composer textarea. Also the mood pill label casing split (lowercase on timeline, Title case on detail).
- Interaction Engineer: the `openedAt` guard in `MessageList.tsx` and the 1.56 overshoot spring are worth feeling live; and `PatternToggle` restarts the whole session on switch (`restart()`), which may read as a jolt mid-breath.
- User & Brand Advocate: replaying `synthesizeDay` over the ten seeded days, Sep 7 and Sep 1 (locked) both close on "I want to remember this one." — a Pip+ user with two bright days a week apart would see the machine repeat itself. The seven free days are distinct. Also check whether "Went for a walk under the amber leaves after lunch. I stopped trying to name it." reads as a memory or a seam to you.
- Accessibility Auditor: the composer's `focus-visible` 3px `amber-ink` outline fires on every tap into the textarea (focus-visible always matches text inputs), so the thread's resting "typing" state is a heavy brown ring (thread-composing-photo-light). Fine for WCAG; check whether it is the look we want.
