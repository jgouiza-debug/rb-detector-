## Red flags
- **Blur used for content-lock, not decoration** — `memory-locked-paywall-light.jpg`: the prior day's card is genuinely blurred (CSS `filter: blur(...)` on placeholder bars, screen title "Saturday, August 29" barely legible through it). This is the one blur/glass-adjacent effect in the app. It reads as functional (signals "locked," not "look how modern I am") and matches design-spec 3.4's own language ("softly locked") — checked and judged *not* a hit, but flagging it explicitly since it's the closest candidate.
- **Composer upload placeholder** — `Composer.tsx:79`: a bare `"…"` glyph on a flat `bg-ink/30` overlay is the only micro-state in the app with no shape/skeleton/icon considered — not a red flag on its own (not a gradient, not template smell) but the weakest link in an otherwise deliberate feedback system. Named under B3, not counted twice here.
- Checked for: default gradients (none beyond the one brand-sanctioned glow), untouched Tailwind spacing (95% on 8pt, remaining 4px steps are component-internal per brief's own carve-out), font/weight/size sprawl (gate: 6/3/3, all within cap), repeated card-grid filler (timeline cards are content, not filler), generic SaaS copy (none found), gratuitous animation (none beyond the two items above, both purposeful). **None found** beyond the two noted above, which I judge non-blocking.

## Top 3 fixes (highest leverage first)
1. Replace the `"…"` photo-upload overlay (`Composer.tsx:79`) with a small ring/skeleton consistent with `Skeleton.tsx` — it's the one spot where a spinner-era default sits next to genuinely considered feedback everywhere else.
2. On `timeline-light.jpg`, quiet the header stack: "your story" (32px), "TODAY'S KEEPSAKE" label, mood pill, and "Monday, September 7" (24px serif) all land in one screenful. Consider moving the mood pill to sit inline with the date rather than opposite the eyebrow label, so there are three visual weights instead of four.
3. Confirm and log actual onboarding completion time in an analytics event — C4's <60s claim in the spec is currently unverifiable from either pixels or code; a simple timestamp-delta check would let a future round certify it instead of assuming it.
